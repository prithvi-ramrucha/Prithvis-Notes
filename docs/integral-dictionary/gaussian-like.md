# Gaussian Integrals
Last Edited: 22/08/2026

The most famous Gaussian integral, 

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{-\alpha x^2} dx = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\alpha}}
$$

where $\alpha$ can be any complex number. A related Gaussian-like integral is,

$$
\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-x^2} \text{sin}(\alpha x^2)}{x^2} dx = 2 \sqrt{\pi \sqrt{1 + \alpha}} \ \text{sin} \left( \frac{1}{2} \text{arctan}(\alpha)  \right)
$$

The integrand exhibits fresnel-type oscillations contained within a Gaussian envelope. Its functional form is similair to those seen in optics (diffraction patterns) but is unphysical.
